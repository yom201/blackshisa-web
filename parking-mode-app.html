Parking mode explained

What parking mode means when the camera is a spare phone.

A parking mode app turns a phone you already own into a focused camera for short parked-car monitoring sessions. The useful question is not whether it replaces every dedicated dash cam, but whether its triggers, recording window, placement, and operating-system requirements match the situation you want to document.

Parking mode is an event-focused recording workflow

A spare-phone setup watches a selected view while the vehicle is parked. BlackShisa can react to motion, sound, or impact rather than asking you to search one uninterrupted recording. When a selected trigger fires, the saved event includes the three seconds immediately before detection, helping preserve the approach or sound that preceded the trigger.

You select a post-trigger duration of 10, 30, or 60 seconds. A shorter window can be easier to review for brief contact, while a longer window may show a person or vehicle leaving the scene. No setting can guarantee that a useful face, plate, or contact point will be visible; distance, light, reflections, obstruction, and camera angle all matter.

  • Three seconds are retained before a detected event
  • Choose 10, 30, or 60 seconds after the trigger
  • Test the actual view before leaving the vehicle

Start with one clear monitoring goal

Decide what you are trying to see before mounting the phone. A forward view may help with a vehicle that reverses toward the bumper. A side-facing view is more relevant for a neighboring door or shopping cart. One phone records one camera direction, so aiming at every possible risk at once usually produces a wide but less useful image.

Place the phone in a stable holder without blocking the driver's required view or interfering with controls, vents, or airbags. Clean the interior and exterior glass in front of the lens, disable unnecessary notifications, check available storage, and record a short test while standing where an incident would likely occur. Reframe if the important area is too small or hidden by reflections.

  • Prioritize one side or approach
  • Keep the mount stable and the lens unobstructed
  • Check framing in the same lighting expected during use

Match detection to the event you care about

Motion detection is useful when a person, door, cart, or vehicle crosses the chosen view. Sound detection can add context for a scrape, slam, or nearby disturbance, although busy streets and car parks can also be noisy. Impact detection responds to movement measured by the phone, but a light touch may not move the mounted device enough to trigger it.

Treat the three trigger types as tools with different blind spots. A passing pedestrian may produce motion without any damage. A loud vehicle may produce sound without approaching your car. Wind, vibration, and movement inside the vehicle can also create unwanted events. Run a supervised test, adjust sensitivity if the app offers that control, and review whether the chosen combination captures the intended scenario.

iPhone and Android require different operating habits

On iPhone, monitoring runs in the foreground and the screen must remain on. Locking the phone, switching away from the app, or allowing the display to turn off does not fit that operating model. Plan power, brightness, and session length accordingly, and confirm that the live monitoring view remains active before you leave the car.

On Android, monitoring uses a foreground service and depends on the permissions granted on that device. Camera, microphone, notifications, battery management, and other system controls can affect operation. Android vendors apply different power-saving behavior, so complete the permission flow, keep the service notification present, and test the exact phone instead of assuming another model's behavior will match.

  • iOS: app in the foreground and screen on
  • Android: foreground service, subject to granted permissions
  • Verify the monitoring state on the device you will leave in the car

Save locally first, then decide what to keep

BlackShisa stores event recordings on the phone first. That makes an offline parking session possible, but it also means storage space and physical access to the device matter. Review events after returning, preserve any relevant original file, and avoid repeatedly editing or re-encoding the only copy if you may need to show what the camera recorded.

Google Drive upload is optional rather than required for detection and local recording. If you enable it, confirm the correct account, connection, and upload status instead of assuming every file has left the phone. Parking rules, recording rules, and acceptable evidence practices vary by place; this guide explains product use and does not provide legal advice.

FAQ

Questions about this setup

Direct answers, including the constraints that matter before you rely on a spare phone.

Does parking mode record continuously?

BlackShisa is designed around detected events. Motion, sound, or impact can trigger a clip containing three seconds before detection and your chosen 10-, 30-, or 60-second period after it.

Can I lock an iPhone while monitoring?

No. On iPhone, the app must remain in the foreground and the screen must stay on for monitoring.

Do I need internet access in the car?

Local event recording does not require a Drive upload. Google Drive is an optional destination, and its upload still depends on account and network conditions.
