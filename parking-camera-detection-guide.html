Select the signal your incident can produce

Choose the trigger that matches the incident you want to capture.

Motion, sound, and impact do not describe three levels of the same detector. They observe different signals: change in the camera image, audio near the microphone, and physical movement reaching the phone. The best setup starts with the event you expect, then tests whether that event actually produces the chosen signal from the mounted position.

Motion

Useful for an approaching person, opening door, cart, or vehicle that visibly crosses the selected camera area.

Sound

Useful for a knock, scrape, slam, or nearby disturbance, while also responding to unrelated voices, traffic, and weather.

Impact

Useful when physical movement travels through the vehicle and holder to the phone, but less reliable for a light or distant touch.

Motion detects image change, not damage

Motion detection is often the earliest cue because it can respond while a person or vehicle approaches. It is especially relevant when the important object remains visible: a neighboring door swinging into the gap, a cart crossing a side view, or a car entering the adjacent bay. Good placement and a clean, stable image matter before any sensitivity choice.

Movement in the frame does not mean contact occurred. Pedestrians, shadows, reflections, trees, rain, headlights, or activity in a busy lane can create events. Conversely, an incident behind a pillar or outside the selected view may cause no visible change. Define the area of interest through placement, then test the natural background activity of that parking space.

  • Can begin while the subject approaches
  • Depends on a visible and stable camera view
  • May react to harmless environmental change

Sound detects an acoustic moment, not its source

Sound detection can mark the time of a door slam, scrape, knock, broken-object noise, or other disturbance near the phone. It may be useful when the contact point is partly hidden but the microphone is close enough to hear it. Window position, cabin insulation, microphone condition, and surrounding noise affect what reaches the device.

The same detector can react to conversations, alarms, shopping carts, motorcycles, rain, wind, or another car's door. A sound clip alone does not identify which object made the noise or whether your vehicle was damaged. Use the audio trigger to locate a time window, then examine the visible sequence and neighboring clips rather than naming a cause from sound alone.

Impact detects movement at the phone, not every contact with the car

Impact detection uses physical movement measured at the device. A strong enough bump transmitted through the body, cabin, and mount can create an event. A secure holder is needed for a stable view, but the same mechanical path can damp a light touch. Contact at a distant corner may not move the phone enough to cross the selected detection threshold.

Ordinary vibration can also trigger an event: a heavy vehicle passing, a door closing elsewhere, wind rocking the car, or someone entering the vehicle while testing. Do not hit the car to calibrate it. While present, use only harmless movement and confirm the resulting file. Treat the absence of an impact event as absence of that measured signal, not proof that nothing happened.

  • Requires vibration to reach the mounted device
  • May miss light or mechanically isolated contact
  • Can react to non-damaging movement

Combine signals around a useful recording window

Using two or three relevant detectors can reduce reliance on one signal, but it also increases the number of clips. Choose from the likely incident: motion plus sound for a door or cart in view; motion plus impact for vehicle approach and transmitted movement; sound plus impact when the visual path is limited. The combination is a search aid, not a guarantee.

Every triggered BlackShisa clip includes three seconds before detection and your selected 10, 30, or 60 seconds after. Save locally first, then optionally upload to Google Drive. FAQ

Questions about this setup

Direct answers, including the constraints that matter before you rely on a spare phone.

Which detector should I turn on first?

Start with the signal your event is most likely to produce and that the mounted phone can observe. For a visible approach, motion is often a useful first test; add others only after testing.

How does higher sensitivity affect the event list?

Higher sensitivity can add unrelated events without improving framing or detail. Adjust only after a controlled test of the actual target and background.

Why are there three seconds before the trigger?

The pre-event interval can preserve the beginning of an approach, movement, or sound that occurred just before detection, providing context for the triggered moment.
