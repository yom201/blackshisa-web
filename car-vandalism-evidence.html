Document activity around a parked car

Record the moments around vandalism or a break-in attempt.

Damage from scratching, tampering, or an attempted break-in may involve a person remaining close to one area of the car rather than a single large impact. A useful setup focuses on the likely approach and working area, handles low-light reflections, and records enough context before and after a detected event to understand the sequence.

Choose the likely working area

Prioritize a door handle, window, mirror, charging port, or exposed panel instead of trying to cover the entire vehicle.

Test after dark

Streetlights, interior reflections, tinted glass, and passing headlights can change a usable daytime view completely.

Preserve behavior and timing

Keep clips that show approach, time spent near the car, and departure, while separating visible actions from interpretation.

Frame the place where activity would happen

For a concern about a side window or door handle, a close side view is more useful than a distant panorama. If the risk is near the front emblem, mirror, or charging connector, place that feature and the path leading to it in the same frame. One spare phone cannot cover every surface, so prioritize a known pattern or the most exposed area.

Include enough surrounding space to see a person approach and leave, but avoid a view so wide that hand movement becomes invisible. Mount the phone stably without obstructing driving visibility, controls, or airbags. Make a harmless test in which someone approaches, pauses near the target area, and steps away; then judge the stored file at normal playback size.

  • Show the target area and its approach path
  • Keep hands or objects large enough to see
  • Accept that one camera has a limited direction

Night conditions must be tested at night

A daytime preview does not reveal what tinted glass, street lighting, a bright dashboard, or the phone display will do after dark. Clean the glass, bring the lens closer to it, reduce avoidable cabin light, and check whether reflections hide the target area. Headlights crossing the frame can briefly change exposure and make nearby details harder to distinguish.

Do not assume adding more light will always improve the recording. Light reflecting from glass, trim, or a nearby object may wash out the foreground, while areas beyond it remain dark. Test at the intended parking location when you can remain with the vehicle. If faces or objects are unclear in the test, describe the setup as contextual monitoring rather than identification.

Select triggers for approach, noise, and physical movement

Motion detection can respond to a person entering or moving within the frame, including time spent near a handle or panel. Sound detection can respond to scraping, glass noise, or a disturbance but also to conversation and traffic. Impact detection responds only when enough movement reaches the phone, so quiet tampering or a scratch may not create a detectable shock.

When an event is triggered, BlackShisa keeps three seconds before detection and the selected 10, 30, or 60 seconds after it. A longer post-trigger window may capture repeated activity or departure, while the pre-event part may show the first approach. Test the chosen combination and expect some unrelated events in a busy or visually active location.

  • Motion can show approach and time near the car
  • Sound can add context but cannot identify an action
  • Impact may miss activity that does not move the mounted phone

Keep originals and distinguish observation from conclusion

Review events around the time damage was noticed and preserve relevant original files on the device. An optional Google Drive upload can create another destination when configured, but verify that it completed. Record separate photos of damage and surroundings. A clip showing someone near the car is not automatically proof that the person caused a mark that is outside the frame.

On iPhone, monitoring requires BlackShisa in the foreground with the screen on. Android uses a foreground service and remains subject to camera, microphone, notification, battery, and other granted permissions. Plan power, storage, heat, device visibility, privacy, and local rules before the session. This page offers operational guidance, not a guarantee of prevention or legal advice.

FAQ

Questions about this setup

Direct answers, including the constraints that matter before you rely on a spare phone.

Can the camera identify anyone who approaches the car?

Not reliably in every setting. Identification depends on face angle, distance, motion, lighting, glass, obstruction, and the phone's image quality. A clip may provide context without a clear identity.

Is impact detection enough for vandalism?

No. Scratching or tampering may not move the phone. Motion can cover visible activity and sound may add another cue, but every trigger also has false or missed events.

How should I think about leaving the phone visible?

There is no guarantee of deterrence. A visible device may also attract attention, so weigh device security and local conditions before leaving it in view.
