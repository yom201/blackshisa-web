Watch the vulnerable side

Watch the side of your parked car for door dings and cart contact.

Door dings happen in a narrow space, often outside the view of a forward-facing camera. A side-focused spare phone can document a neighboring door opening, a shopping cart passing, and the sound or movement around a possible contact, provided the important zone is visible through the glass.

Map the door swing

Frame the neighboring door edge, the gap between vehicles, and the panel of your car most likely to be touched.

Include the cart route

In retail parking, leave enough view for carts arriving from the aisle without making the contact zone too small.

Look for combined context

Motion, a contact-like sound, and vibration close together can help you locate the relevant clip, but none alone proves damage.

A side view is different from a general parking view

A door ding develops across the small gap between two cars. The useful frame includes the outer edge of the neighboring door before it opens, the space it travels through, and the exposed door or quarter panel of your vehicle. If the lens points only down the parking aisle, the critical door edge may remain outside the image even though the other car is visible.

Park first, sit in the normal cabin position, and decide which side has the tighter gap or more active pedestrian route. Mount the spare phone toward that side without placing it where it can become loose, block necessary driving visibility, or interfere with an airbag. One camera covers one side well; it does not provide a complete view of both sides.

  • Keep the neighboring door edge visible
  • Include the gap and your exposed panel
  • Prioritize one side for each session

Control reflections before adjusting sensitivity

Side glass can mirror seats, clothing, dashboard lights, or the phone's own bright screen. Clean both surfaces of the window, move the lens closer to the glass without pressing it against a moving pane, and adjust the angle until the cabin reflection is less prominent. At night, a bright interior or screen can overpower details outside.

Make a saved test clip with a person safely opening a nearby door without touching your vehicle. Repeat with a cart moving along the likely route. Review whether the door edge remains distinguishable throughout its swing and whether the cart is large enough to identify its direction. Correct the physical view before trying to compensate with more detection events.

Use each trigger for what it can observe

Motion detection can respond as a door enters the frame or a cart moves through it, potentially starting before contact. Sound detection can react to a knock, scrape, or door closing but will also hear unrelated activity. Impact detection relies on movement reaching the mounted phone, so a small dent on a distant panel may not create a strong signal at the device.

A triggered BlackShisa event includes three seconds before detection and your chosen 10, 30, or 60 seconds after it. The pre-event portion may show the door beginning to open; a longer post-event portion may show the person checking the panel or leaving. Select the duration and test the relevant trigger combination before leaving the vehicle.

  • Motion follows visible door or cart travel
  • Sound may locate a moment but not identify its cause
  • Impact depends on vibration transmitted through car and mount

Review the event without overstating what it shows

A useful clip may show an object approaching your panel, a matching sound, and the object moving away. It may also show only part of that sequence. Compare timestamps and adjacent events, keep the original local file, and distinguish clearly between visible contact, possible contact hidden by the door, and simple proximity. The recording should speak for itself.

Files are stored locally first, with optional Google Drive upload when configured and connected. iPhone monitoring requires the app in the foreground and the screen on; Android uses a foreground service subject to the permissions granted. Heat, power, storage, privacy, and local recording rules also deserve attention. This page explains setup, not legal conclusions.

FAQ

Questions about this setup

Direct answers, including the constraints that matter before you rely on a spare phone.

Should the phone face straight out of the side window?

Not necessarily. Angle it so the neighboring door edge, the gap, and your exposed panel stay in view. A short saved test is more reliable than a universal angle.

Will impact detection catch a light door ding?

It may not. A light contact can be damped before vibration reaches the mounted phone, so motion and sound can provide additional event cues.

Can one phone monitor both sides of the car?

One camera direction cannot clearly cover both sides in most cabins. Choose the side with the narrower gap or greater risk for that parking session.
