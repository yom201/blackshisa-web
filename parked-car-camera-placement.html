Placement determines what evidence can exist

Choose a stable angle that shows the risk area clearly.

Detection can only react to what the phone can see, hear, or physically feel. Placement therefore comes before sensitivity. A useful position keeps the expected approach and contact area in one view, limits reflections and obstructions, holds the device securely, and stays clear of the driver's view, controls, and airbag deployment zones.

Front view

Use it for vehicles or people approaching through the front lane, keeping the exposed bumper area and travel path visible.

Side view

Use it for neighboring doors, carts, handles, or side panels, with the lens close to clean glass to reduce cabin reflections.

Rear view

Use it when the rear corner or lane is the priority, after confirming that seats, pillars, tint, and defroster lines do not hide the target.

Begin with the incident path, then choose the window

Imagine the event as a path: where a vehicle, person, door, or cart first enters, where it could come close to your car, and where it would leave. Try to place all three stages inside one frame. The contact area should not sit at the extreme edge, where lens distortion, cropping, or a small shift in the holder can remove it.

A front view suits a bumper-facing lane or a vehicle reversing toward you. A side view suits door swings and activity by a handle or window. A rear view suits traffic or access behind the car. One phone cannot cover the whole perimeter, so re-evaluate the priority after every significantly different parking position.

  • Show entry, closest point, and exit
  • Keep the critical area away from the frame edge
  • Re-aim after changing parking orientation

Control glass, reflections, and obstruction

Clean the phone lens and the window on both sides. Bring the camera close to the glass without pressing on a moving pane or letting the holder touch controls. A gap between lens and window can reveal dashboard, seat, clothing, and screen reflections. Tilting slightly may help, but every change also alters the visible route, so review a saved test afterward.

Check the view in the same light expected during monitoring. Sun can reflect from trim, night lighting can mirror the cabin, and passing headlights can change exposure. Rear seats, headrests, roof pillars, tint, stickers, wipers, and heating lines can obscure details. If an obstruction crosses the likely face, plate, door edge, or contact point, move the camera rather than hoping software will recover it.

Mount the phone as vehicle equipment, not a loose object

Use a holder rated for the phone's size and weight and attach it to a surface the holder is intended to use. Check the joint, clamp, adhesive or suction condition before each session. The mount must not block required road visibility, mirrors, instruments, controls, vents, driver assistance sensors, or the path of an airbag. Remove or reposition it before driving if necessary.

Route any power cable so it cannot tangle with steering, pedals, shifter, seat rails, or doors. Do not force a cable under vehicle trim or alter vehicle wiring without appropriate knowledge. A stable image is important, but device and occupant safety comes first. If the chosen evidence angle conflicts with safe placement, choose another angle or do not use that setup.

  • Use a compatible, inspected holder
  • Keep airbags and driving visibility clear
  • Keep cables away from moving controls and mechanisms

Validate placement with the saved event, not a glance

With the car stationary and you present, have someone travel through the expected route without contacting the vehicle. Test the motion view, an ordinary sound, and a harmless movement if those triggers will be enabled. Open the recorded file and pause at entry, closest approach, and exit. Check detail size, focus, reflections, and whether the mount shifted.

BlackShisa events include three seconds before detection and the selected 10, 30, or 60 seconds after it, saved locally first with optional Google Drive upload. Confirm that the duration contains the route you framed. Also verify iPhone foreground and screen-on operation, or the Android foreground service and permissions. Good placement improves opportunity; it never guarantees complete evidence.

FAQ

Questions about this setup

Direct answers, including the constraints that matter before you rely on a spare phone.

What is the best universal camera position?

There is none. The best position is the safe, stable one that keeps the expected entry path and vulnerable area visible in a saved test for that parking space.

Should the lens touch the window?

Place it close enough to reduce reflections, but do not press against moving glass or create an unstable mount. Clean both surfaces and inspect the test file.

Can a wider angle solve all blind spots?

No. A wider frame can make important details smaller and still cannot see through pillars, seats, vehicles, or opaque bodywork. Prioritize one risk area.
