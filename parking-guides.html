Parking evidence library

Build a parked-car camera setup one decision at a time.

Choose the problem you need to solve. Each guide covers a different decision, from camera placement and trigger selection to hit-and-run evidence and heat safety.

Updated: 2026-08-12

Parking mode explained What parking mode means when the camera is a spare phone. A parking mode app turns a phone you already own into a focused camera for short parked-car monitoring sessions. The useful question is not whether it replaces every dedicated dash cam, but whether its triggers, recording window, placement, and operating-system requirements match the situation you want to document. Prepare before a parking-lot incident Capture useful context when someone hits your parked car and leaves. A camera cannot prevent a parking-lot hit-and-run or guarantee decisive evidence. It can, however, preserve a time-linked view of an approaching vehicle, a possible contact, and what happens next when the phone is aimed well and the relevant trigger responds. Watch the vulnerable side Watch the side of your parked car for door dings and cart contact. Door dings happen in a narrow space, often outside the view of a forward-facing camera. A side-focused spare phone can document a neighboring door opening, a shopping cart passing, and the sound or movement around a possible contact, provided the important zone is visible through the glass. Document activity around a parked car Record the moments around vandalism or a break-in attempt. Damage from scratching, tampering, or an attempted break-in may involve a person remaining close to one area of the car rather than a single large impact. A useful setup focuses on the likely approach and working area, handles low-light reflections, and records enough context before and after a detected event to understand the sequence. Give an old phone a focused second job Turn an old phone into a focused camera for parked-car evidence. A spare phone already contains a camera, microphone, motion sensors, screen, storage, and network connection. Turning it into a parked-car camera is therefore mainly a preparation task: confirm that the device is healthy, remove distractions, grant only the permissions needed, mount it safely, and test the complete path from detection to a saved clip. Choose the tool that fits the parking job When a spare-phone app is—and is not—a practical dash cam alternative. A phone app and a dedicated dash cam can both record around a parked vehicle, but they solve the job differently. A spare phone favors planned, movable, short monitoring sessions with hardware you already own. A fixed camera system favors a more integrated routine. The right choice depends on how often, how long, and how broadly you need to monitor. Choose a workflow your phone can maintain Choose a parked-car monitoring setup that fits iPhone or Android. The camera view and event clips may look similar across phones, but the operating model is not identical. On iPhone, monitoring stays visibly in the foreground with the screen on. On Android, it uses a foreground service and remains subject to granted permissions and device power controls. A reliable session begins by preparing for those differences rather than hiding them. Placement determines what evidence can exist Choose a stable angle that shows the risk area clearly. Detection can only react to what the phone can see, hear, or physically feel. Placement therefore comes before sensitivity. A useful position keeps the expected approach and contact area in one view, limits reflections and obstructions, holds the device securely, and stays clear of the driver's view, controls, and airbag deployment zones. Select the signal your incident can produce Choose the trigger that matches the incident you want to capture. Motion, sound, and impact do not describe three levels of the same detector. They observe different signals: change in the camera image, audio near the microphone, and physical movement reaching the phone. The best setup starts with the event you expect, then tests whether that event actually produces the chosen signal from the mounted position. Protect the device, vehicle, and occupants Plan parking-monitoring sessions around battery, heat and visibility. A parked vehicle can become a harsh environment for electronics, especially in direct sun. A spare-phone camera should therefore be treated as a temporary setup that you inspect and actively manage, not as equipment that can be left indefinitely. Device health, temperature, power, mounting, storage, and visibility all belong in the decision to start or stop a session.
