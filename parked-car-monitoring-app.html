Choose a workflow your phone can maintain

Choose a parked-car monitoring setup that fits iPhone or Android.

The camera view and event clips may look similar across phones, but the operating model is not identical. On iPhone, monitoring stays visibly in the foreground with the screen on. On Android, it uses a foreground service and remains subject to granted permissions and device power controls. A reliable session begins by preparing for those differences rather than hiding them.

iPhone remains visible

Keep BlackShisa in the foreground and the display on; locking the device or switching apps does not meet the monitoring condition.

Android depends on permissions

Confirm the foreground-service notification and the camera, microphone, notification, and relevant power permissions on that exact device.

Both need a real event test

A preview is not enough: trigger an event, open the stored file, and confirm the pre- and post-trigger sequence before leaving.

The iPhone workflow is intentionally foreground and screen-on

Start monitoring only after the phone is mounted, power is planned, and the target area is framed. Keep BlackShisa as the visible app and prevent the display from turning off during the session. Do not press the lock button, open another app over it, or treat a dark screen as evidence that monitoring continues in the same way.

Because the display remains on, use only the brightness needed to confirm operation and consider battery condition, charging safety, heat, and session length. Silence unrelated alerts where appropriate so they do not distract from the monitoring view. Immediately before leaving, look for the active monitoring state and then perform the same check again when you return.

  • BlackShisa stays in the foreground
  • The iPhone display remains on
  • Power and heat must be planned for the visible session

Android uses a foreground service, not identical background behavior

Complete the app's permission flow for the functions you intend to use. Camera and microphone access support visual and sound detection, notifications make the foreground service visible, and device-specific battery controls may influence continued operation. If a permission is denied or later removed, do not assume the corresponding feature continues unchanged.

Android manufacturers and system versions can present different menus and power-saving behavior. Confirm the service notification, run a test after a normal restart, and check whether the app remains allowed under the phone's battery settings. Avoid relying solely on a guide written for another model. The useful answer is the observed result on the actual spare phone.

  • Foreground service remains visible through a notification
  • Operation is subject to granted permissions
  • Test after restart and after changing power settings

Detection and saved-event structure are shared concepts

On either platform, choose motion, sound, or impact according to the incident you want to capture. Motion relies on change inside the camera view, sound on audio near the microphone, and impact on physical movement reaching the device. Each can create unrelated events and each can miss an event that does not produce its particular signal.

A triggered recording contains three seconds before detection and a selected 10, 30, or 60 seconds after it. Files are saved locally first. Google Drive is optional and requires a configured account and available connection for upload. Verify a stored event on the phone and, if used, verify Drive separately; one successful step does not prove the other.

Use one cross-platform departure checklist

For either phone, inspect battery health, available storage, mount stability, lens and glass, target framing, focus, reflections, and expected cabin temperature. Create motion, sound, and harmless vibration tests relevant to your chosen triggers. Confirm that the resulting file shows the useful area and the timeline you selected, not merely that a detector indicator changed.

Then apply the platform-specific check: foreground app plus lit screen on iPhone, or visible foreground service plus required permissions on Android. No operating system setting guarantees a useful recording, and neither platform can overcome an obstructed or poorly lit view. Follow local privacy and recording rules; this is product-operation guidance rather than legal advice.

  • Shared: view, storage, power, heat, mount, event file
  • iPhone: foreground app and screen on
  • Android: foreground service and granted permissions

FAQ

Questions about this setup

Direct answers, including the constraints that matter before you rely on a spare phone.

Can BlackShisa monitor with an iPhone screen off?

No. The iPhone workflow requires BlackShisa in the foreground and the screen on.

Does Android monitoring continue with every permission denied?

No. The foreground service and detection functions are subject to the permissions granted and to device settings. Complete and test the required configuration.

Are event clips different on iPhone and Android?

The core event concept is the same: motion, sound, or impact can trigger three seconds of pre-event context plus a selected 10, 30, or 60 seconds afterward.
